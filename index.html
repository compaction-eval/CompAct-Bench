CompAct-Bench A Benchmark for Working‑Context Compaction in Long‑Horizon Agent Tasks

§1 · Overview

What survives the rewind?

Long-horizon agents accumulate actions, observations, failed attempts and partial progress until the working context nears its window. Modern harnesses then ask an LM to compact that history into something shorter. CompAct-Bench turns this into a controlled experiment: start from a trajectory the executor already solves, cut it, let a compactor rewrite the past at 10% of its length, and check whether the same executor can still finish.

Abstract

Long-horizon agents increasingly rely on context compaction as growing interaction histories approach model context limits. Most existing systems delegate this process to an LM that converts the accumulated history into a shorter working context, yet the compaction capability of current models remains poorly understood. To this end, we introduce CompAct-Bench, a 200-task process-level benchmark spanning information seeking, software engineering, and workspace automation through BrowseComp, SWE-bench, and an internal CompanyBench suite. For each instance, an evaluated LM compacts the history at an intermediate boundary, and a fixed executor resumes from the corresponding environment state under a matched action budget. We evaluate twelve frontier LMs as compactors. Although every retained task is solved in all three pre-compaction screening runs, average post-compaction accuracy falls to 56.7%, and the best model reaches only 68.5%. Failure analysis reveals two pathways: omissions lead to repeated work and budget exhaustion, while state distortion can turn tentative evidence into conclusions and trigger premature termination. By making working-context compaction measurable as an execution-critical model capability, CompAct-Bench provides a foundation for building long-horizon agents that remain reliable across context-window boundaries.

History length does not explain it. State location does.

Later boundaries always hand the compactor a longer history, yet Search gets worse and Coding gets better. A repository keeps edits on disk; search evidence lives only in the conversation and must survive inside Mb.

Two ways to lose the thread

Failed continuations from all twelve compactors were audited unit by unit against Hb. Omission makes the agent redo what it already did. Distortion makes it believe the wrong state, and stop.

§6 · Compactor seat

Your turn: compact the history.

You are Cφ. Pick fragments of the agent's history, choose how to phrase them, and stay inside the 10% capacity. Then the executor resumes from your Mb with a fixed action budget. Shorter phrasings are cheaper, and sometimes they quietly change what the agent believes.
