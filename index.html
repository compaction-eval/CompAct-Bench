The one-question story

    
Every task was solved three times out of three.
Then its history was compacted to 10%.

    
Each of the 200 instances begins from a trajectory the executor already finishes in every screening run. Cut it at 25%, 50% or 75%, hand the history to a compactor, and let the same executor resume under the same action budget. On average, only 56.7% of continuations still land.

    

    
Overview of CompAct-Bench (Fig. 1, animated). Successful trajectories are compacted at intermediate boundaries, and the executor must complete the original task t from Mb alone.

  







  
01
The problem

  

    
§1 · The problem

    
A shorter context can still lose the plot.

    
Long-horizon agents accumulate actions, observations, failed attempts and partial progress until the working context approaches the window limit. Modern harnesses then ask an LM to compact the history into something shorter. That is not document summarization: what matters is not what was important in the past, but what must be carried forward for the next action.


    

      
The compaction task · history before the boundary, and its compacted form

      $$H_b = (a_1, o_1, \dots, a_b, o_b), \qquad M_b = C_\phi(H_b;\, r), \qquad r_{\text{actual}} = \frac{\ell(M_b)}{\ell(H_b)}$$

The protocol

  

    
§2 · The protocol

    
A controlled rewind, then a fresh run.

    
Everything except the compacted context is frozen. The task, the reconstructed environment at the boundary, the executor and the action budget are identical across compactors, so Mb is the only variable. No reference summaries, no compaction labels: the task outcome is the metric.

04
Results

  

    
§4 · Results

    
The tape gets thinner. The task gets harder.

    
At a 10% retention target, every frontier compactor loses execution-critical state. Overall accuracy spans 46.0% to 68.5%. Strength partially transfers across domains, but rankings reshuffle: switch the channel and watch the board re-sort.

Stage matters, in opposite directions

    
Averaged over compactors, Search falls from 52.9% at the 25% boundary to 34.6% at 75%, while Coding rises from 64.0% to 86.1%. Workspace peaks in the middle. Hover a model to trace it across all three panels.

05
Where state lives

  

    
§5 · Task-state persistence

    
History length does not explain it. State location does.

    
Later boundaries always hand the compactor a longer reel. Yet Search gets worse and Coding gets better. The difference is where progress is stored: a repository keeps edits on disk, while search evidence exists only in the conversation and must survive inside Mb.


    

      
Average Hb tokens given to the compactor · reel area ∝ tokens

      

    


    

      

        
Coding · the repository is a second tape

        
Continuing from the reconstructed boundary state, with no history at all, already beats starting over by up to +56.3 points. Adding Mb helps on top of it at 50% and 75%.

        

      

      

        
Search · the context is the only tape

        
Without Hb, search continuations collapse to 7.4% at the late boundary. Mb buys a steady +26–27 points at every stage: the evidence lives nowhere else.

        

      

    

  







  
06
Failure modes

  

    
§6 · Failure modes

    
Two ways to lose the thread.

    
Failed continuations from all twelve compactors were audited by comparing Hb with Mb unit by unit. Omission makes the agent redo what it already did. Distortion makes it believe the wrong state, and stop.
